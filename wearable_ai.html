Sliding-Window Streaming VLMs on Wearable AI

Oct 12, 2026 in progress videostreaminginteractionrecurrent memorylatency

Qwen3.8-27B and Muse Glimmer 30B (8-frame window + self-written memory) on all 2,100 Wearable AI validation sessions with the official starter kit (with and without the 300 s rule), and EgoProactive under the Pro2Bench protocol.

EfficiencyEgo-MC-BenchWearable AI

Setup

  • Data: all 2,100 validation sessions of the starter kit, 700 per task.
  • EgoLongQA: one multiple-choice question at the end of the video; the answer is one letter.
  • EgoConv: a free-text answer at the end of each question's interval, with the model's own earlier answers as history.
  • EgoProactive: at the end of every ~8 s chunk, $interrupt$ <message> or $silent$, given the gold dialogue (last 4 turns).
  • Pro2Bench: EgoProactive also run with the No-Plan zero-shot prompts of the Pro2Bench paper (App. G), greedy for both models.
  • Frame rate / window: 1 fps; the last 8 frames (8 s, long edge ≤ 672 px) plus a self-written memory, instead of the whole past video.
  • Qwen3.8-27B: thinking off (greedy) and thinking low (reasoning_effort=low, official thinking sampling).
  • Muse Glimmer 30B: reasoning_strength=low, official sampling; reported on SGLang + DFlash, plain-vLLM run also shown.

Metrics

  • EgoLongQA · accuracy: share of questions answered with the correct letter.
  • EgoConv · BLEU-4: word overlap (1- to 4-word phrases) between each answer and the reference answer, averaged over turns.
  • EgoConv · LLM judge: Llama-4-Maverick scores each answer against the reference as correct (1), partly correct (0.5) or wrong (0), averaged over turns.
  • EgoProactive · macro / G-mean F1: each chunk is a two-way decision (interrupt or stay silent); F1 is computed for each class, then averaged (macro) or multiplied and square-rooted (G-mean), so always interrupting or always staying silent scores low.
  • EgoProactive · interrupt / silent recall: share of chunks that should get an interruption that do, and share of chunks that should stay silent that do.
  • 300 s rule: the challenge's compute limit; a session whose estimated inference time on one H200 exceeds 300 s gets an empty prediction and scores 0.

EgoProactive (Pro2Bench paper protocol)

System prompt App. G.2, verbatim
You are a helpful, proactive AI assistant guiding a user through a step-by-step procedural task. You observe the user’s activity through egocentric video from their perspective.

Your responsibilities:
1. Track which step the user is currently performing.
2. Detect when a step is completed and proactively announce the next step.
3. Detect mistakes or deviations from the expected procedure:
 - Skipped steps
 - Wrong step ordering
 - Technique errors
 - Substitutions or improvisations
4. When a deviation is detected, suggest a recovery action.
5. If no guidance is needed (user is progressing normally), stay silent.

Output format:
- If the user needs guidance (step completed, mistake detected, or question asked), respond with: $interrupt$ followed by your guidance.
- If no guidance is needed, respond with exactly: $silent$

Be concise. Focus on what the user should do next, not what they just did.
User message after the chunk's 8 frames; No-Plan duplex template, visual summary = our memory
Here is the summary of the recent visual context: {visual_summary}
Here is the most recent conversation between the user and the assistant:
User: {user_utterance}
Assistant: {assistant_response}

Takeaways

  • Without a time limit, Qwen leads all three tasks: EgoLongQA 0.64–0.66 vs 0.49–0.52, EgoConv judge 0.46–0.55 vs 0.34–0.36, EgoProactive macro F1 0.48–0.49 vs 0.36–0.43.
  • The 300 s rule decides which setups are usable: Qwen thinking low and Muse on plain vLLM exceed it on most sessions and score near 0; Muse on SGLang + DFlash stays within it most often and leads EgoLongQA (0.451 vs 0.411) and EgoProactive (0.276 vs 0.243), while Qwen thinking off keeps EgoConv.
  • EgoProactive depends on the prompt: with the starter-kit prompt, Muse interrupts almost always (silent recall 0.02–0.10); with the Pro2Bench prompt, Qwen thinking low reaches G-mean F1 0.635, second only to the paper's fine-tuned planner system (0.68), Muse 0.53–0.54, Qwen thinking off 0.26.
  • Serving stack matters here, unlike Ego-MC: on plain vLLM Muse mostly skips reasoning on EgoProactive decisions (median 3 vs 112 tokens), and macro F1 falls from 0.43 to 0.36; the cause is not identified.
