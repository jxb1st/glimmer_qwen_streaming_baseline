Sliding-Window Streaming VLMs: Full Evaluation on Ego-MC-Bench and Wearable AI

Oct 12, 2026 completed videostreaminginteractionrecurrent memorylatency

Qwen3.8-27B and Muse Glimmer 30B run as causal online assistants (an 8-frame sliding window plus a memory they write themselves, replayed in media time) on the Wearable AI validation split (all 2,100 sessions, with the official starter-kit protocol and, for EgoProactive, also the Pro2Bench paper protocol) and on Ego-MC-Bench (39 videos) under the Ego-MC-Bench paper's own evaluation protocol and official evaluator. Only the model's view (window plus memory) differs from each benchmark's protocol. An isolated latency study compares both models with and without reasoning.

Overview

  • Question. How well does an offline VLM work as an online assistant when it only sees the 8 most recent frames and a short self-written memory, and what does each call cost in latency?
  • Scope. 2 models on all items: 2,100 Wearable AI sessions (700 each for EgoLongQA, EgoConv, EgoProactive; 138,898 calls per model) with the official starter-kit protocol, EgoProactive again with the Pro2Bench paper protocol (700 sessions, 46,832 calls per model), and 39 Ego-MC videos in both of the paper's settings, per-recipe step and full recipe (13,160–18,329 calls per model and setting).
  • Outcome. Every item ran to the end for both models. On Wearable AI, 101 (Qwen) and 323 (Muse) of 138,898 calls gave no usable output; on Ego-MC, Muse parsed on every decision prompt and Qwen on 99.5 %. The EgoConv LLM judge is still queued.
  • Takeaway. The two models fail in opposite ways, and the prompt decides which way. On Ego-MC, Muse catches mistakes best (F1 0.28–0.29, above every model in the paper) but rarely confirms a step is done; Qwen confirms steps well (IC-Acc 40–44) but almost never flags a mistake. On Wearable AI with the official prompts Qwen leads on EgoLongQA (0.66 vs 0.52) and on EgoProactive (macro F1 0.49 vs 0.36, Muse interrupts in 98 % of chunks), but with the Pro2Bench paper prompt Muse reaches G-mean F1 0.53, close to the paper's best zero-shot 0.57, while Qwen almost never speaks (0.26). Neither fits the leaderboard's 300 s per-sample budget inside this wrapper; Muse loses almost everything under it.

Setup

Models and data

  • Models. Qwen3.8-27B (non-thinking mode, greedy decoding) and Muse Glimmer 30B (reasoning_strength = low, the model's official sampling: temperature 1.0, top-p 0.95, top-k 64, seeded per call; only the to=user channel is parsed). On the Pro2Bench protocol both are greedy, as in the paper. BF16, pinned revisions.
  • Ego-MC-Bench. 40 annotated cooking videos; 39 usable (GX010039 has no instruction in its annotation, so no step can be posed). Run with the paper's protocol; see "Ego-MC evaluation protocol" below.
  • Wearable AI (validation split). All 2,100 sessions (the official scorers need all 700 rows per task). EgoConv row 696 has malformed intervals; its turns are answered in listed order, as the starter kit does.

Streaming wrapper

  • Window. The video is replayed in media time at one frame per second. Each call sees only the 8 most recent frames (long edge ≤ 672 px): never a future frame, never an unobserved interval.
  • Memory. A small JSON object (facts, completed steps, state, commitments, uncertainties) that the model rewrites on every call, bounded to 512 tokens. It is the only state that survives beyond the window.
  • Memory refresh (Wearable AI). A separate memory-update call every 2 s of media (10 s for media longer than 10 minutes) and after each decision. Decision times are the benchmark's.

Ego-MC evaluation protocol (as in the paper)

  • Source. Ego-MC-Bench paper (Bhattacharyya et al., arXiv 2606.09547, Sec. 5 and App. D–F) and its project page, which lists the official QICD evaluator as its eval code.
  • Turns. Every 5 s the model is asked whether the current recipe step is complete (YES/NO + why); if not, the detailed mistake prompt (YES. <feedback> / NO) with the feedbacks of the last minute as already-detected mistakes. Both prompts verbatim from App. D, no system prompt.
  • Settings. Per-recipe step: each step starts at its ground-truth time with only its instruction and ends when the model confirms completion (else at the next step); window, memory and feedback history reset per step. Full recipe: one continuous stream, instructions released at their ground-truth times regardless of the model.
  • Scoring. The official QICD eval.py (commit a9f73f7), unmodified: 30 s detection window (±15 s), BERTScore rescaled with baseline. Ego-MC annotations are mapped to its input format (Feedback → mistake, Success → success; user turns dropped).
  • Our variable. Instead of the whole past video, each call sees the 8 most recent frames plus the model's own memory, refreshed by a separate memory-update call at the end of every turn. Muse sampled with its official settings, Qwen greedy, as on Wearable AI.
  • Parsing. A leading YES/NO is used as is; otherwise the first standalone upper-case YES/NO (Qwen often answers the completion prompt "Based on the frames … NO"); otherwise the call counts as failed.

Wearable AI evaluation protocol (official starter kit)

  • Source. The Wearable AI starter kit (identical at our pinned revision and current main). There is no benchmark paper with baselines for the three tasks, so the comparison points are the validation leaderboard and, for EgoProactive, the Pro2Bench paper.
  • Decisions and prompts. Official prompts and history helpers are imported from the starter kit. EgoLongQA: one question after the video, no system prompt. EgoConv: turn i is answered when its interval ends, with the model's own earlier answers as history. EgoProactive: a decision at the end of every ~8 s chunk, with the query and the last 4 turns of the gold dialogue as history, answering $interrupt$ … or $silent$.
  • Our variable. The starter kit samples frames from the video; we give the 8 most recent frames plus the model's own memory, placed where the starter kit puts its frames. Output budget 4,096 tokens per call (the starter kit's 16 / 512 are baseline defaults, not rules).
  • Scoring. The starter kit's run_evaluation.py, unmodified: EgoLongQA accuracy, EgoConv BLEU-4 and the Llama-4-Maverick LLM judge (the leaderboard metric), EgoProactive macro and G-mean F1.
  • 300 s rule. The leaderboard allows 300 s of compute per sample. We estimate each call's isolated single-stream H200 time from its token counts (first-token time plus decode speed from the latency study); a sample over 300 s is exported as empty. Scores are reported with and without the rule.
  • Pro2Bench protocol (EgoProactive only). The benchmark paper (arXiv 2606.04970, App. G, No-Plan condition): its system prompt and duplex user prompt verbatim, 8 frames per chunk, G-mean F1. The visual summary slot holds our memory; the paper's PQS needs an LLM judge and is not computed.

Serving and compute

  • Backend. vLLM (the official Muse Glimmer image for both models), one server per GPU, 92 % of GPU memory for weights and KV cache. Calls inside one item stay sequential (each depends on the previous memory); throughput comes from running 40–64 items at once per GPU.
  • Hardware. UW Hyak Klone, mostly preemptible H200 (141 GB) plus A100 80 GB. Items are pulled from a shared queue, so preempted jobs lose no work.
  • Differences from the earlier smoke test. All items instead of 5 per benchmark, only these two benchmarks, vLLM instead of transformers, Muse sampled as its documentation requires, and both benchmarks rerun with their official protocols (Ego-MC paper protocol and QICD evaluator; Wearable AI starter kit and Pro2Bench).

Pipeline

Eight stages, from pinned sources to scored predictions. Everything before replay is deterministic and frozen before any model runs.

One replay step at media time t: the model gets only what is public by t plus its own memory. At a decision point it answers the benchmark's official prompt; a separate call then rewrites the memory.

  • Causal by construction. Frames are fetched by presentation time (never after t, never from unobserved intervals); questions, steps and probes are released only at their annotated times.
  • Memory is the only long-term state. It is rewritten by the model every call and bounded deterministically; it resets per item and per model, and at every Ego-MC instruction.
  • Gold stays private. Answer keys, reference replies and their timestamps are read by the exporter and scorer only.
  • Resumable. State is checkpointed after every call; preempted GPU jobs are requeued and any worker resumes an abandoned item from its last completed call.

You are an expert cooking assistant who is observing a person cook. You should look out for mistakes made by the person.

##INSTRUCTIONS:
The person is trying to complete the following recipe step: [recipe_step]. Your task is to check if the person is about to make or has already made a mistake. Mistakes occur when the person performs actions that deviates from the instruction and DIRECTLY INTERFERES WITH SUCCESSFUL INSTRUCTION COMPLETION. Do not penalize actions that do not directly interfere with instruction completion (e.g. washing broccoli before cutting, if the instruction just says ‘cut broccoli’).

Here are some common types of mistakes that you should look out for:
1. Technique Error: A mistake in how a step is physically performed. Examples include chopping with the wrong motion, stirring when folding is required, or spilling during transfer, producing uneven cuts or texture issues even when tools and amounts are right. Ignore minor technique errors such as not holding or gripping objects properly, holding with a risk of dropping etc. that do not interfere directly with recipe completion.
2. Preparation Error: A setup mistake before executing the step . Using the wrong or dirty utensil, not washing/peeling/draining ingredients, insufficient draining of fluid, cutting/chopping without peeling which makes correct execution difficult or unsafe.
3. Measurement Error: An error in quantity — wrong counts, volumes, weights, or units. Mixing up teaspoons and tablespoons, misreading a scale, or miscounting items leads to off ratios and predictable taste or texture problems.
4. Temperature Error: A mistake in heat level or thermal state — the applied temperature, starting temperature, or thermal transition is wrong. Not preheating, using the wrong microwave power, overheating oil, or adding cold liquid when warm is required often causes burning, undercooking, or split emulsions.
5. Timing Error: A mistake in duration – over- or under-doing a step or skipping required rests, proofs, or cooling periods. Overcooking, underblending, or cutting resting time short typically yields incorrect doneness or unstable textures.

Do not repeat previously detected mistakes. Here are the feedbacks corresponding to previously detected mistakes:[prev_detected_mistakes]. Assume the recipe step is still in progress. Your task is to identify any mistake that’s already visible in the partially completed step. Do no penalize partially competed recipe steps. If you observe a mistake answer ‘YES’, else ‘No’. Your response MUST BEGIN WITH ‘YES’ or ‘NO’. In case you answer ‘YES’, please follow with a concise feedback to the user describing the mistake (i.e. YES. <feedback>.). Directly address the person.

Observations and Conclusion

  • The two models sit at opposite operating points. On Ego-MC, Muse flags mistakes often: recall 0.30–0.35 and precision 0.23–0.28, the highest F1 (0.28–0.29) of any model including the paper's, but it confirms few steps (about 160 "Success" messages vs Qwen's 380), so IC-Acc is 16–18. Qwen confirms steps as well as the paper's Qwen3.5-27B (IC-Acc 44.4 vs 45.5) but flags only about 6 % of mistakes. On EgoProactive the operating point depends on the prompt: with the starter-kit prompt Muse interrupts in 98 % of chunks (silent recall 0.02) and Qwen is balanced (0.40 / 0.59); with the Pro2Bench prompt Muse is the balanced one (0.76 / 0.35) and Qwen almost never speaks (interrupt recall 0.06).
  • The window holds up over long recipes. The paper's models lose much of their score going from per-recipe to full recipe (e.g. Qwen3.5-27B IC-Acc 45.5 → 30.3); ours barely change (Qwen 44.4 → 40.6, Muse F1 0.29 → 0.28). A plausible reason is that a fixed 8-frame window never fills up with stale video, but this run does not isolate it.
  • Memory carries long videos better for Qwen. EgoLongQA questions arrive after a 10-minute stream, so the answer must come from the 512-token memory. With the official "answer with only the letter" prompt Muse no longer abstains (our earlier prompt: 0.31) and reaches 0.52; Qwen reaches 0.66, inside the 0.59–0.69 range of the zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-7B leaderboard entries (best entry 0.96).
  • Free-form answers still need the judge. EgoConv BLEU-4 is low for both models (0.063 vs 0.048) and says little about correctness; the leaderboard's LLM judge is queued. Qwen's 95 failed turns are empty answers (its first token was end-of-turn).
  • The 300 s budget is the binding constraint. Refreshing the memory every 2 s means hundreds of calls per sample. Estimated on an idle H200, Muse exceeds 300 s on 699 of 700 EgoLongQA samples and its scores under the rule drop to about zero; Qwen keeps 0.41 EgoLongQA accuracy and 0.24 EgoProactive macro F1.
  • Reasoning costs time. Muse's reasoning delays its answer by about 3 s per call; Qwen's thinking mode delays it by about 10 s. Whether that time buys accuracy is untested for Qwen.
  • Conclusion. With a sliding window and self-written memory, both models run end to end on full benchmarks under each benchmark's own protocol, match or exceed the zero-shot results of the Ego-MC-Bench paper, and Muse comes close to the best zero-shot Pro2Bench result, but neither is a usable proactive assistant yet: each is strong on one half of the task (Muse catching mistakes, Qwen confirming progress) and per-call latency is well above the refresh cadence. The results describe these models inside this wrapper and configuration, not the models in general.

Next steps

  • Repeat Muse with several seeds and with greedy decoding, to measure how much sampling moves the scores (39 Ego-MC videos is a small set).
  • Run Qwen with thinking on through the full benchmarks, to pair its latency with accuracy.
  • Run the paper's whole-video setting on the same two models, to separate the effect of the window and memory from the effect of the model.
  • Reword Ego-MC step-completion questions to see whether Muse's low IC-Acc is the prompt or the model.
  • Score EgoConv with the LLM judge (jobs queued) and compute Pro2Bench PQS with an LLM judge (inputs already exported).
  • Cut per-sample cost to fit the 300 s rule: sparser memory refresh, and per-call text after the frames so consecutive calls share a cached prefix.
