  • DFlash halves Muse's latency: 6.7 → 3.1 s per call, answer after 2.0 s instead of 3.7 s.
  • Same plain decode speed for both models (62–66 tok/s, first token 0.44–0.51 s): on one engine, latency is set by how many tokens a model writes.

Muse Serving Stack

Meta documents vLLM + its reasoning parser as the NVIDIA production path and DFlash speculative decoding (5-layer block-diffusion drafter, block size 5) for SGLang, llama.cpp and ExecuTorch (deploy, spec-decode). ExecuTorch serves one request and one image at a time, so it cannot run this protocol.

Compute Budget

Wearable AI allows 300 s of inference per sample. Each call's isolated H200 time is estimated from its tokens and the measured speed of its setup (Qwen 64.7 tok/s, Muse 62.5 tok/s plain vLLM / 145.6 tok/s with DFlash); over-budget samples score as empty.

  • With DFlash, Muse fits the budget on 641 / 700 EgoLongQA samples (plain vLLM: 1 / 700); scores under the rule are on the Wearable AI page.
  • Under load (32–64 concurrent items per GPU) calls are several times slower than in isolation; DFlash's gain shrinks with batching and was not measured separately.

Observations and Next Steps

  • Muse's per-call speed advantage is entirely in serving (speculative decoding), not in the model.
  • No configuration meets a 2 s refresh cadence on one H200 (best: 3.1 s per call).

Next

  • Give Qwen its own official speed-up (e.g. multi-token-prediction speculative decoding) and re-measure.
  • Throughput at fixed concurrency (1 / 8 / 32 streams) with and without DFlash.
  • Accuracy–latency trade-off across reasoning levels (Qwen off / low, Muse low / medium).
