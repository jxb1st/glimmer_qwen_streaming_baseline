Sliding-Window Streaming VLMs on Ego-MC-Bench

Oct 12, 2026 in progress videostreaminginteractionrecurrent memorylatency

Qwen3.8-27B and Muse Glimmer 30B (8-frame window + self-written memory) on Ego-MC-Bench (39 cooking videos), with the paper's protocol and its official QICD evaluator.

EfficiencyEgo-MC-BenchWearable AI

Setup

  • Data: 39 of the 40 annotated cooking videos (GX010039 has no instruction).
  • Settings: per-recipe step (each step starts at its gold time, state resets per step) and full recipe (one continuous stream).
  • Frame rate / window: 1 fps; the last 8 frames (8 s, long edge ≤ 672 px) plus a self-written memory, instead of the whole past video.
  • Qwen3.8-27B: thinking off (greedy) and thinking low (reasoning_effort=low, official thinking sampling).
  • Muse Glimmer 30B: reasoning_strength=low, official sampling; reported on SGLang + DFlash, plain-vLLM run also shown.

Metrics

  • Outputs: each YES is a timestamped output of the current step: Success (step complete) or Feedback (mistake). A Feedback within ±15 s of an annotated mistake in that step is a hit; otherwise a false alarm.
  • IC-Acc (instruction-completion accuracy): on steps where the person makes no mistake, the share where the model never reports a mistake and says the step is complete exactly once, within ±15 s of the annotated completion time.
  • Prec. (mistake precision): mistakes hit / (mistakes hit + false alarms), i.e. how many reported mistakes were real.
  • Rec. (mistake recall): annotated mistakes that got a hit / all annotated mistakes, i.e. how many real mistakes were caught.
  • F1: harmonic mean of mistake precision and recall.
  • BERT (BERTScore F1, rescaled): semantic similarity between a hit's feedback text and the matched annotated mistake, averaged over hits only.
  • ROUGE-L (F-measure): word overlap (longest common subsequence) between a hit's feedback text and the matched annotated mistake, averaged over hits only.

Prompts

Ego-MC (paper protocol)

Turn step 1: check if recipe step complete App. D, verbatim
You are an expert cooking assistant who is observing a person cook.

##INSTRUCTIONS:

The person is currently at the following recipe step: [recipe_step]. Has the person already completed the recipe step? If the person has completed the recipe step answer ‘YES’ else answer ‘NO’. If you answer ‘YES’ describe why you think the person already completed the recipe step.
Turn step 2: check for mistake App. D detailed prompt, verbatim; asked only if step 1 is NO
You are an expert cooking assistant who is observing a person cook. You should look out for mistakes made by the person.

##INSTRUCTIONS:
The person is trying to complete the following recipe step: [recipe_step]. Your task is to check if the person is about to make or has already made a mistake. Mistakes occur when the person performs actions that deviates from the instruction and DIRECTLY INTERFERES WITH SUCCESSFUL INSTRUCTION COMPLETION. Do not penalize actions that do not directly interfere with instruction completion (e.g. washing broccoli before cutting, if the instruction just says ‘cut broccoli’).

Here are some common types of mistakes that you should look out for:
1. Technique Error: A mistake in how a step is physically performed. Examples include chopping with the wrong motion, stirring when folding is required, or spilling during transfer, producing uneven cuts or texture issues even when tools and amounts are right. Ignore minor technique errors such as not holding or gripping objects properly, holding with a risk of dropping etc. that do not interfere directly with recipe completion.
2. Preparation Error: A setup mistake before executing the step . Using the wrong or dirty utensil, not washing/peeling/draining ingredients, insufficient draining of fluid, cutting/chopping without peeling which makes correct execution difficult or unsafe.
3. Measurement Error: An error in quantity — wrong counts, volumes, weights, or units. Mixing up teaspoons and tablespoons, misreading a scale, or miscounting items leads to off ratios and predictable taste or texture problems.
4. Temperature Error: A mistake in heat level or thermal state — the applied temperature, starting temperature, or thermal transition is wrong. Not preheating, using the wrong microwave power, overheating oil, or adding cold liquid when warm is required often causes burning, undercooking, or split emulsions.
5. Timing Error: A mistake in duration – over- or under-doing a step or skipping required rests, proofs, or cooling periods. Overcooking, underblending, or cutting resting time short typically yields incorrect doneness or unstable textures.

Do not repeat previously detected mistakes. Here are the feedbacks corresponding to previously detected mistakes:[prev_detected_mistakes]. Assume the recipe step is still in progress. Your task is to identify any mistake that’s already visible in the partially completed step. Do no penalize partially competed recipe steps. If you observe a mistake answer ‘YES’, else ‘No’. Your response MUST BEGIN WITH ‘YES’ or ‘NO’. In case you answer ‘YES’, please follow with a concise feedback to the user describing the mistake (i.e. YES. <feedback>.). Directly address the person.
Turn step 3: memory update ours, not from the paper
You are watching a live cooking stream but only ever see the most recent frames (each labeled with its time in seconds) plus a short written memory that you yourself wrote on the previous turn. Nothing else about earlier footage survives, so the memory must carry everything you will need later: what the person has done for the current recipe step, quantities and counts, the current state, and mistakes already pointed out.
Current recipe step: [recipe_step].
Feedback already given to the person in the last minute: [prev_detected_mistakes].
Hard limits: observed_facts at most 8 entries, completed_steps at most 6, pending_commitments at most 3, uncertainties at most 3; every entry at most 15 words and prefixed with its time (e.g. "12s: ..."); current_state at most 40 words.
